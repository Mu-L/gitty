## Why it matters

**Why do this?**

Gitty reached the point where the window could get crowded — four panes plus a terminal that splits — and hiding panes is what a history browser needs to breathe. Shipping it on npm makes the tool installable the way people actually grab desktop utilities.

**What problem does it solve?**

A pane you are not using still takes its share of the screen; the hiding feature lets the window be reduced to just the diff, or just the log, and restored in one click. On the distribution side, a standalone binary was awkward to install, and the empty space under a hidden pane wasted real estate a working layout could use.

**What does it affect?**

An installable npm package (0.1.2) with a `gitty` command, a global command from `run.sh`, and a window where every pane can hide and come back — with per-repository state and running shells preserved.
